# Learning Outcomes Contracts: Module 2: The Digital Twin (Gazebo & Unity)

This document outlines the expected learning outcomes and interaction patterns for students completing each chapter within Module 2. These serve as "contracts" between the educational content and the learner, defining the skills and knowledge gained.

## Chapter 1: Digital Twins and Physics-based Simulation - Learning Contract

**Title**: Digital Twins and Physics-based Simulation

-   **Expected Outcome**: Upon successful completion of this chapter, the student will be able to:
    -   Define what a digital twin is in the context of robotics and AI.
    -   Describe the core principles of physics-based simulation and its importance for robotics.
    -   Explain the role of simulation environments (e.g., Gazebo, Unity) in creating digital twins.
-   **Student Interaction**:
    -   Read and comprehend conceptual explanations of digital twins and simulation.
    -   Interpret diagrams illustrating the architecture of digital twin systems.
    -   Engage with self-assessment questions to test understanding of foundational concepts.

## Chapter 2: Gazebo — Physics, Gravity, Collisions, and Sensors - Learning Contract

**Title**: Gazebo — Physics, Gravity, Collisions, and Sensors

-   **Expected Outcome**: Upon successful completion of this chapter, the student will be able to:
    -   Explain and configure basic physics properties within Gazebo (e.g., gravity settings, object mass).
    -   Understand how collision shapes and materials affect robot interactions in simulation.
    -   Describe the principles of sensor simulation within Gazebo (e.g., LiDAR, depth cameras, IMUs).
    -   Interpret simulated sensor data outputs.
-   **Student Interaction**:
    -   Read and analyze Gazebo physics configuration examples.
    -   Examine how different collision geometries impact simulation.
    -   Study examples of sensor model definitions and their parameters.
    -   Analyze conceptual examples of using simulated sensor data.

## Chapter 3: Unity — High-Fidelity Rendering and Human–Robot Interaction - Learning Contract

**Title**: Unity — High-Fidelity Rendering and Human–Robot Interaction

-   **Expected Outcome**: Upon successful completion of this chapter, the student will be able to:
    -   Conceptualize how Unity is used for creating high-fidelity visual rendering in digital twin environments.
    -   Understand principles of designing human–robot interaction within a Unity simulation.
    -   Describe how Unity assets contribute to the realism and functionality of a digital twin.
-   **Student Interaction**:
    -   Read and analyze Unity scene setup examples, focusing on lighting, materials, and textures.
    -   Examine conceptual designs for user interfaces and interaction flows with a simulated robot.
    -   Understand the role of scripting in Unity for dynamic simulation elements and HRI.
